## Market structure (89)

## Pricing and valuation (69)

- API Gravity — Inverted density scale for crude where higher means lighter oil; drives refined product yield, refinery fit, and grade price differentials
- ATM — Option strike set at the forward rate, carrying maximum time value and zero intrinsic; the headline vol that anchors the entire FX surface
- Backwardation — Downward sloping curve where deferred delivery trades below nearby, signaling tight near term supply and paying long positions positive roll yield
- Backwardation Roll Gain — Profit from rolling a long future when the front sits above the deferred; a persistent commodity carry return that mirrors FX carry
- Ballast Leg Economics — Pricing the empty repositioning voyage into the freight quote so round trip TCE, not the laden rate, reflects true vessel earnings; often halves the daily figure
- Barrier Option — Exotic whose payoff switches on or off when spot touches a level (knock in or knock out); cheaper than a vanilla and the dominant FX exotic flow
- Basis — Difference between 2 reference prices, usually spot minus futures or across locations and grades; the raw material of every relative value trade
- Bunkers, IFO versus MGO, and the Bunker Hedge — Marine fuel grades split by sulphur and refining, 40 to 60 percent of voyage cash cost; hedged with gasoil futures or port index swaps that carry basis risk
- Butterfly — Average 25 delta wing vol minus ATM vol, measuring smile curvature; a volga trade that prices tail risk and widens ahead of binary events
- Call Option — Right to buy at a fixed strike for a premium; the buyer risks only the premium for convex upside while a naked writer faces unbounded loss
- Carry Cross Asset — Systematic factor harvesting the yield of high over low assets across FX, rates, commodities, and equities; positive on average but exposed to correlated crash unwinds
- Carry Trade — Borrow a low rate currency to fund a higher rate one and pocket the differential; profitable on average, subject to violent unwinds when risk aversion spikes
- Cash to 3 Month Spread — LME cash minus 3 month forward, the single most important gauge of base metal physical tightness and a leading indicator for flat price direction
- CFD Crude — Weekly spread between Dated Brent and front month Brent futures; the most sensitive real time read on physical crude tightness, not a retail FX CFD
- Contango — Upward sloping forward curve where deferred futures trade above nearer ones; passive longs bleed negative roll yield rolling up the curve
- Contango Carry Metals — Rent, insurance, and financing cost that caps LME contango width; when contango exceeds carry, a cash and carry arb pulls the spread back
- Contango Roll Cost — Loss from rolling a long future when deferred sits above nearby; the hidden drag that makes commodity ETFs bleed even as spot rises
- Convenience Yield — Non monetary benefit of holding physical inventory, inferred from the spot futures gap; it spikes on supply scarcity and drives backwardation
- Cost of Carry — Total holding cost of storage, insurance, and financing minus convenience yield linking spot to futures; position versus full carry starts every calendar spread
- Covered Interest Parity — No arbitrage law pinning FX forwards to the rate differential; persistent post 2008 violations create the cross currency basis hedgers pay
- Crop Year — The 12 month marketing window starting at harvest that organizes WASDE balance sheets and defines old crop versus new crop spreads
- Cross Currency Basis — Deviation of FX swap pricing from covered interest parity; persistently negative versus USD, it is the true cost of dollar hedging and a funding stress gauge
- Crude Quality — Density and sulfur content define each crude grade, setting refinery economics and the tradeable quality differentials between light sweet and heavy sour
- Dated Brent — Spot assessment for physical North Sea BFOET cargoes loading 10 to 25 days out; it anchors most global crude formulas, with the CFD spread signaling physical tightness
- Digital Option — All or nothing option paying a fixed amount if spot finishes past the strike; the simple payoff hides extreme near expiry gamma and unhedgeable pin risk
- EFS — Brent futures minus Dubai swaps, the spread linking Atlantic and Asian crude benchmarks that governs when East to West arbitrage cargoes flow
- Equilibrium Price — The supply demand balancing level every mean reversion trade targets; a moving anchor whose structural shifts are the biggest risk to reversion bets
- Floating Storage — Storing crude on tankers when contango exceeds charter plus financing; both a contango profit strategy and an AIS trackable leading indicator of oversupply
- Forward Curve — The strip of futures prices across all delivery dates; the market's pricing of time and the substrate for every commodity relative value trade
- Forward Points — Pips added to spot to build the FX forward rate from the interest rate differential; they are the carry a hedger or carry trader pays or receives
- Freight — Seaborne shipping cost quoted in Worldscale; the gating variable deciding whether cross basin crude arbitrage is economic, and one of the most volatile inputs in commodities
- Geopolitical Repricing Sequence — Oil shocks reprice in a fixed order of vol, skew, flat price, then curve; reading the sequence separates real disruption from headline noise and times the trade

- Gold Silver Ratio — Gold price divided by silver, the classic precious metals relative value metric; readings above 80 favor a long silver short gold reversion over 6 to 18 months
- Interest Rate Differential — The gap between two countries' benchmark rates, the main driver of FX forward pricing and carry; its expected direction moves spot more than its level

- Lease Rate — Interest rate for borrowing physical gold, computed as dollar rate minus GOFO; spikes flag physical scarcity and looming short squeezes
- London Fix — Twice daily LBMA electronic auction setting the benchmark gold price that settles ETFs, derivatives, and reserves; concentrated flow creates tradeable patterns
- Market Hire — Prevailing daily time charter rate you mark open period freight positions against; the freight forward curve compressed into one number
- Moneyness — Relationship between an option's strike and spot spanning ITM, ATM, OTM; sets the intrinsic versus time value split and approximates delta
- Old Crop New Crop — Price spread between current harvest and next harvest futures; a direct read on present tightness versus expected future balance sheets
- OTM — Options with zero intrinsic value and pure time value; cheap convex tail hedges and premium harvesting fodder driven entirely by implied vol
- Payoff Diagram — Plot of a position's profit against underlying price at expiry; the 4 building block shapes that compose every multi leg strategy
- Physical Premium — Price paid above the exchange benchmark for delivery at a specific location; reveals regional tightness that flat futures cannot show
- Premium — Upfront price a buyer pays for an option, equal to intrinsic plus time value; the buyer's maximum loss and seller's maximum gain
- Process Margin — Value of outputs minus cost of inputs earned by whoever runs the conversion asset; the single shape behind crack, crush, and spark spreads

- Quality Spread — Price gap between 2 crude grades differing in API gravity or sulfur; trades refinery economics and regulation with lower vol than flat price

## Microstructure (26)

- Adverse Selection — The market maker's risk that a quote is a free option to better informed counterparties; the single largest cost in FX making, managed through tiering, last look, and markout
- Aggregated Data — Tick events compressed into OHLCV bars for medium frequency research, trading path detail for tractability; bar size and boundary alignment materially shift backtest results
- Bid-Ask Spread — The gap between best bid and ask that prices immediacy; every round trip costs at least one spread, dominating cost for high turnover spread traders
- Client Tiering — Charging each client a spread scaled to their historical markout toxicity; the primary OTC defense that lets dealers keep profitable uninformed flow while pricing informed flow out
- Depth of Book — Resting order volume at every price level beyond top of book; the primary input for estimating large order market impact and a warning signal when depth evaporates before news
- Flow Signals — Predictive signals from the direction and urgency of customer order flow; banks seeing 10 to 15% of global FX detect macro shifts days before price, outsiders reconstruct weaker versions from ECN tapes
- Half Spread — Half the bid ask spread, the cost of a single trade from mid; the fundamental unit of FX transaction cost analysis and a maker's gross revenue per side before adverse selection
- Inventory — The net position a market maker accumulates as a byproduct of quoting, not by choice; flattening it quickly while earning the spread is the central making challenge
- Liquidity — The ease of trading size without moving price, measured by spread, depth, volume, and Kyle's lambda; concentrated in front months and evaporates precisely when you need to exit
- Market Impact — The price movement your own trade causes, split into reversing temporary and lasting permanent components; scales as the square root of size over ADV and caps tradeable position size
- Markout — Post trade price movement measured over a fixed horizon; the primary tool for scoring execution quality, flow toxicity, and market making profitability
- Mid Price — Arithmetic average of best bid and ask; the TCA benchmark for execution quality, but misleading when the book is thin or skewed
- Positioning Signal — Contrarian read of aggregate net long or short data from COT and surveys; extreme positioning flags crowded trades vulnerable to violent unwinds
- Quote — A price shown to the market signaling willingness to buy or sell at that level; the fundamental act of market making
- Quoting — Continuously streaming bid and ask prices as the core market maker activity; an engine skews quotes for inventory, volatility, and adverse selection
- Round Trip — A buy plus a sell of the same instrument; you pay half a spread per leg, setting the minimum cost any strategy must beat
- Skew Microstructure — Asymmetry between bid side and ask side order book volume; one of the most reliable directional predictors at horizons under 10 seconds
- Slippage — The gap between intended and actual fill price from spread crossing, impact, and delay; the difference between a backtest and reality
- Sniping — Hitting a maker's stale quote before it refreshes for near risk free profit; the edge is latency, not fundamental value
- Stale Quote — A price not refreshed after the market moved; a guaranteed loser for the quoter and the primary source of sniping losses
- Sweep — A large aggressive order consuming multiple book levels at worsening prices; the primary market impact mechanism and a signal of informed urgency
- Tick Data — Every trade and quote update recorded with microsecond timestamps; the raw material for microstructure research, demanding heavy storage and noise handling
- Top of Book — The best bid and ask forming the BBO, the tightest immediate execution price; silent on available size beyond the first units
- Toxic Flow — Client flow that consistently predicts price moving against the maker who filled it; the empirical face of adverse selection measured through markouts
- Turnover — How frequently positions are flattened and reestablished; it multiplies round trip cost and sets the per trade alpha hurdle a strategy must clear
- Two Way Price — A simultaneous bid and ask shown together, forcing the counterparty to declare direction; the standard convention of market making

## Risk (27)

- Basis Risk — Residual risk left when a hedge instrument differs from the exposure by location, grade, or timing; it converts flat price risk into spread risk
- Bunker Risk — Marine fuel price moving against you between fixing a voyage and bunkering; at 40 to 60 percent of voyage cost, it dominates P&L
- Carry Unwind — Rapid simultaneous reversal of carry trades on a volatility spike; funding currencies like JPY surge, correlations converge, and months of income vanish in days
- Convexity — A nonlinear payoff that gains disproportionately from large moves while losing little on small ones; the shape of risk that decides tail survival
- Correlation Breakdown — Sudden shift in cross asset correlations during stress that pushes diversified positions to lose together; the tail risk that undermines risk parity
- Daily Loss Limit — Caps how much a desk can lose in one session before trading stops; typically sized at 3 to 5 times daily PnL volatility
- Expected Shortfall — Average loss on the days worse than VaR; captures the tail depth VaR ignores and runs 2 to 3 times VaR for fat tailed commodities
- Freight Basis Risk — Residual gap left after hedging a physical lane with an index FFA; the wedge between your route and the Baltic basket where hedged freight PnL lands
- Freight Rate Risk — Exposure of PnL to changes in the cost of moving cargo by sea; the gating variable that decides whether a location arbitrage clears
- Gap Risk — Price jumps discontinuously past where a stop would fill, so realized loss is a multiple of intended risk; only options or being flat protect
- Hard Limit — Absolute system enforced threshold that fires the kill switch and may auto flatten on breach; the last line before a firm ending loss
- Hedging — Offsetting position that trades large price risk for smaller basis risk plus cost; distinct from relative value by intent, not instrument
- Implied Volatility — Market's forward looking price variability backed out of option prices; running 1 to 4 points above realized vol is a persistent premium for sellers
- Inventory Risk — Risk that price moves against the inventory a market maker builds while quoting; managed by skewing quotes and fast flattening, not stops
- Kill Switch — Emergency mechanism that mass cancels every resting order and blocks new ones in under 100 ms; the panic button that prevents a Knight Capital event
- Leverage — Controlling large notional with a sliver of posted capital; a WTI contract runs near 13 times capital, amplifying both PnL and forced liquidation risk
- Liquidity Risk — Cannot exit a position at a reasonable price when needed; market and funding illiquidity feed each other and turn hedged spreads into naked bets
- Margin — Collateral posted to guarantee futures performance; falling below maintenance triggers a call, and forced deleveraging amplifies moves beyond fundamentals
- Maximum Drawdown — Largest peak to trough equity decline before a new high; asymmetric recovery makes it the binding survival constraint, since 50 percent lost needs 100 percent regained
- Overnight Risk — Holding through closed or thin hours where you cannot react; combines gap, event, and session liquidity risk, sized at 25 to 50 percent of intraday limits
- Position Limit — Maximum contracts held long or short across exchange, regulatory, and internal tiers; prevents cornering and caps single bet loss, smallest limit binds
- Soft Limit — Warning threshold at 60 to 80 percent of the hard limit; the yellow light that buys time to cut exposure before a forced kill switch fire sale
- Structural Break — Permanent, testable shift in a relationship's parameters; the enemy of mean reversion, since the old mean is gone and reverting to it loses money
- Tail Risk — Rare extreme moves far outside what a normal distribution predicts; fat tails make 8 to 10 sigma commodity events routine, favoring convex protection over diversification
- Value at Risk — Single number for the loss threshold not breached on X percent of days; a fence around normal losses that says nothing about how far you fall beyond it
- Volatility — Standard deviation of returns and the denominator of trading; sets position size, spread valuation, and option prices; clusters and mean reverts
- Volatility Smile — Pattern of implied vol across strikes at one expiry; its shape reveals which direction the market fears, with commodities often showing positive call skew

## Macro (23)

- Central Bank Buying — Sovereign gold accumulation above 1,000 tonnes per year since 2022; a price insensitive demand floor that broke the real rates gold link
- Central Bank Intervention — A central bank buying or selling its own currency to steer the rate; produces gap moves that stops and risk models cannot survive
- Central Bank Policy — Rate and balance sheet tools that set the cost of money; hits commodities through the USD, demand, and financing channels
- Chokepoint — Narrow maritime straits carrying most seaborne oil; the largest source of geopolitical risk premium, worst when spare capacity is thin
- Commodity Currencies — Exchange rates of resource exporters that track their dominant export; a liquid FX proxy for expressing commodity views
- DXY Correlation — Persistent inverse link between the trade weighted dollar and USD priced commodities; a regime filter that supply shocks can override
- Geopolitical Risk — The risk premium markets pay for potential supply disruptions; small and persistent but asymmetric, best traded through out of the money options
- Green Metals — Copper, nickel, lithium, cobalt, and rare earths critical to electrification; policy driven structural demand running against slow mine supply
- Intervention — Central bank entering FX to move its own currency; the primary tail risk in carry trades, gapping violently through stops
- Macro Regime — Growth and inflation combined into 4 quadrants; the highest level strategic call that determines which asset classes and factors work
- Monetary Policy Divergence — Central banks moving rates in opposite directions; the strongest persistent FX trend driver, reversing violently when the rate gap narrows
- Petrocurrency — Currency of an oil exporter linked to crude through the trade balance; NOK, CAD, RUB, BRL, MXN as tradeable oil proxies
- Real Interest Rates — Nominal rates minus inflation, quoted directly by TIPS yields; the dominant driver of gold and the opportunity cost for all assets
- Real Rates Regime — The prevailing level and direction of inflation adjusted rates; a single dial carrying -0.7 to -0.9 correlation with gold
- Regime Shift — A fundamental change in the data generating process; correlations and vol reset, breaking strategies calibrated on the prior regime
- Risk-On Risk-Off — Markets oscillating between chasing risk and fleeing to safety; cross asset correlations spike as everything moves as one risk factor
- Safe Haven Assets — Instruments that hold value in crises through negative tail correlation; gold, Treasuries, JPY, CHF, USD, with initial liquidation risk
- Spare Capacity — Idle oil production that can restart within 90 days; the buffer that caps prices and sizes the geopolitical risk premium
- Supply Shock — Sudden change in commodity availability forcing rapid repricing; the temporary versus permanent duration call decides whether to fade or trend
- Terms of Trade — The ratio of a country's export prices to import prices; the strongest fundamental predictor of commodity linked currencies, with backtest Sharpe near 0.5
- Terms of Trade Shock — A sudden large swing in a country's export to import price ratio from commodity moves; the currency adjusts over 3 to 12 months, creating persistent tradeable FX trends
- TIPS Breakeven — Nominal yield minus TIPS yield, the market's expected inflation; separates real rate moves from nominal, the distinction that drives gold
- Yield Curve Slope — The 10Y minus 2Y spread (2s10s), the market's cleanest cycle gauge; inversion leads recessions and four steepening or flattening regimes read the growth and policy path

## Quantitative (28)

- Charm — The rate delta drifts as time passes with spot unchanged; leaves a Friday hedged book non neutral by Monday and dominates 0DTE delta management
- Color — The rate gamma changes as time passes; balloons the hedging cost of any short option in its final week and drives explosive 0DTE gamma growth
- Correlation — The linear co movement between 2 assets from -1 to +1; foundation of portfolio risk and spread sizing, unstable and spiking toward 1 in crises
- Delta — Option price change per $1 spot move and approximate ITM probability; the hedge ratio and the axis organizing the FX vol surface
- Gamma — The rate of change of delta, the payoff curvature; how delta hedged options monetize realized versus implied vol and the core risk of short option books
- Greeks — The full family of option risk sensitivities from delta to third order; a dashboard mapping how price responds to spot, vol, time, and rates
- Kurtosis — Tail fatness of a return distribution versus normal; why parametric VaR and Black Scholes understate the frequency of extreme commodity and EM FX moves
- Mean Reversion — The tendency of a price or spread to return to its long run average; foundation of spread trading, valid only for stationary cointegrated series
- Momentum Cross Asset — Systematic time series momentum buying recent winners and shorting losers across assets; the core CTA factor, strong in trends and painful at regime turns
- Observation Date vs Forward Date — The two date axes of a forward price panel; keying the wrong one turns curve shape into fake returns and silently corrupts every risk number
- Realized Volatility — The actual annualized standard deviation of past returns; the backward looking benchmark against implied vol that defines the vol risk premium
- Rho — Option price sensitivity to the risk free rate; a rounding error for short dated options but material for multi year FX and gold options through the forward
- Risk Parity — Portfolio construction sizing each asset to contribute equal risk; levers low vol bonds and cuts gross exposure across the book when vol spikes
- Sharpe Ratio — Excess return per unit of return standard deviation; the primary performance metric, scalable by position size, and almost always lower live than in backtest
- Skewness — The asymmetry of a return distribution; commodities skew positive on supply spikes while FX carry skews negative with violent unwinds
- Speed — The rate gamma changes as spot moves; explodes near barriers and drives the asymmetric hedging flow behind expiry pinning
- Stocks to Use Ratio — Ending inventory divided by annual consumption; the primary tightness gauge for grains, with a convex price relationship that spikes below critical thresholds
- Theta — The daily decay of an option's time value; the rent a holder pays or a seller collects, accelerating into expiry as the direct tradeoff to gamma
- Ultima — Third order vol greek, the rate volga changes with vol; negligible in calm markets but dominates deep OTM repricing when vol explodes
- Value Cross Asset — Contrarian systematic factor buying below fair value and selling above across FX, commodities, and bonds; slowest factor, naturally hedges momentum crash risk
- Vanna — Cross greek measuring delta sensitivity to vol; drives FX dealer rehedging flows from exotic books that predictably push spot 20 to 50 pips
- Vega — Option price sensitivity to a 1 point implied vol move; the dominant risk on any options book and vol's primary tradeable exposure
- Veta — Rate at which vega bleeds as time passes; thins long vol tail hedges and drives calendar spread P&L even with vol unchanged
- Vol of Vol — Volatility of volatility itself; the tail P&L driver that justifies smile curvature and destroys short vol books when vol regimes flip
- Vol Surface — 2D map of implied vol across strike and tenor, built in FX from ATM, risk reversal, and butterfly; the pricing engine for every exotic
- Volga — Second order greek measuring vega sensitivity to vol; gives OTM wings convexity and sets butterfly pricing, the vanilla way to trade vol of vol
- Z-Score — Number of standard deviations a reading sits from its mean; the primary mean reversion entry signal that normalizes spreads across units
- Zomma — Cross greek dGamma/dVol; shifts gamma from ATM toward the wings as vol rises, compounding subsequent spot losses on short OTM positions

## Strategies (25)

- Arabica-Robusta Spread — Trades the quality gap between Arabica (KC) and Robusta (RC) coffee; roaster substitution anchors mean reversion, but Brazil frost can blow it open.
- Brent-WTI Spread — Prices the gap between seaborne Brent and landlocked WTI at Cushing; anchored by transportation economics, it mean reverts unless infrastructure regimes shift.
- Calendar Spread — Buys one delivery month and sells another of the same commodity, isolating forward curve shape while cancelling most directional price risk.
- Cash and Carry Arbitrage — Buy spot, store it, and sell a future to lock a fixed carry profit; this arb caps contango, with storage the binding constraint.
- Cocoa NY-London Spread — Trades ICE US cocoa minus London cocoa converted to dollars, capturing bean quality differentials, GBP/USD moves, and regional grind demand
- Crack Spread — Refiner's gross margin, refined product value minus crude cost; both a margin hedge and a real time read on crude demand
- Crush Spread — Soybean processing margin, meal plus oil value minus bean cost; the agricultural twin of the crack spread, quoted in cents per bushel
- Gasoline Crack — RBOB minus crude refining margin, the most seasonal energy spread; widens into US summer driving season from spring lows
- Gold-Copper Ratio — Gold divided by copper as a single fear versus growth gauge; rising ratio signals fear, falling signals industrial optimism
- Heating Crack — Middle distillate minus crude refining margin, ULSD or gasoil; widens on Northern Hemisphere winter heating and tracks industrial diesel demand
- Henry Hub-TTF Spread — US versus European gas price gap, anchored by the full chain LNG netback cost that decides whether cargoes sail to Europe
- Inter-Commodity Spread — Long one commodity, short a related one; profits from relative pricing anchored by processing, substitution, or shared demand linkage
- KC-Chicago Wheat Spread — Hard red winter minus soft red winter wheat, the market price of milling protein and Southern Plains drought; reverts only at harvest
- Location Arbitrage — Same commodity priced at different locations; the spread is bounded by transport cost and closes when physical flows respond
- Platinum-Palladium Spread — Two metals doing the same catalytic converter job; substitution at price extremes gives the spread a structural ceiling and floor
- Relet — Charter a ship in at fixed hire, employ it out on voyages, and pocket the TCE minus hire margin; structurally long freight
- Scrubber Spread — VLSFO minus HSFO marine fuel spread (Hi5), the per tonne saving that decides whether a scrubber retrofit pays or strands capex
- SHFE vs LME Arb — Chinese versus international base metal prices after VAT, freight, and FX; the import parity window driving physical metal flows into China
- Soybean-Corn Spread — Soybean divided by corn, the acreage battle ratio that mean reverts through spring planting decisions hitting supply 6 months later
- Spread Trade — Simultaneous long and short in 2 related contracts, profiting from the price difference not direction; lower volatility, margin, and risk
- Straddle — Long ATM call plus put at one strike, the purest volatility bet; the benchmark FX option quote at each tenor
- Strangle — Long OTM call plus put at 25 delta strikes, cheaper than a straddle but needs a bigger move; the butterfly building block
- Strategies — Map of content hub linking every strategy playbook in the vault, grouped by type, separating executable trades from their underlying concepts.
- Sugar-Ethanol Spread — Brazilian mill mix, sugar parity versus ethanol parity per tonne of cane, carrying a hidden oil beta through the pump
- Variance Swap — Pure realized variance bet with no direction, delta, or theta; squaring returns means one shock dominates and destroys the short

- Dollar Index — Weighted geometric mean of USD against 6 currencies, 58% EUR, unchanged since 1973; the daily read on broad dollar strength with inverse correlation to commodities.

- ETF Flows — Daily change in physical metal held by precious metal ETFs like GLD; the visible investment demand gauge that separates real money rallies from fragile speculative ones.

- ICCO — Intergovernmental cocoa body publishing quarterly grindings and annual surplus or deficit forecasts; its deficit revisions steepen the curve into backwardation, cocoa's answer to WASDE.
- ICE Cocoa Stocks — Certified cocoa in ICE warehouses deliverable against futures, the tightest measure of immediately available supply; falling stocks near expiry drive front month backwardation.
- IEA — Paris consumer side agency whose monthly Oil Market Report drives medium term positioning; demand and supply revisions move 6 to 24 month calendar spreads, not the front.
- LME Warehouse Stocks — Daily visible base metals inventory in LME approved warehouses; rate of change and the cancelled warrant ratio flag physical demand and looming squeezes before metal moves.
- VIX — CBOE index of expected 30 day SPX volatility from a model free option strip; the fear gauge and risk on risk off thermometer that triggers cross asset carry unwinds above 25.
- VVIX — Implied volatility of VIX options, the vol of vol; a high VVIX with calm VIX signals quiet tail hedging that often precedes a volatility spike and carry unwind.
- WASDE — USDA monthly global crop balance sheet released near the 10th; the ending stocks and stocks to use figures routinely swing corn, wheat, and soybeans 3 to 5% within minutes.
- West African Port Arrivals — Weekly cocoa bean arrivals at Ivory Coast and Ghana ports, the highest frequency supply indicator; cumulative shortfalls versus prior seasons front run ICCO production estimates.

## Infrastructure (24)

- API — Structured message interface connecting trading systems to exchanges, brokers, and data providers, where FIX and WebSocket carry order flow while slow REST handles reference data
- Branch Prediction — CPU guesses which side of a conditional runs; each misprediction costs 5 to 7 ns, and data dependent pricing branches defeat it unless rewritten branchless
- Cache Locality — Laying out data contiguously so the CPU reads from 1 ns cache rather than 100 ns RAM; arrays over linked structures keeps the hot path fast
- Cache Miss — CPU stall when requested data is absent from cache; an L3 miss to RAM costs roughly 100 ns and can double tick to trade
- Co-location — Placing servers inside the exchange data center to cut round trip latency to microseconds versus tens of milliseconds remote; table stakes for electronic market making
- Cold Path — The periodic non latency critical part of a system handling risk, P&L, and reporting in Python, feeding recalibrated parameters to the hot path
- Data Feed — Live exchange stream of prices, sizes, and trades; Level 1 shows top of book while Level 2 shows full depth, and stale feeds trade on phantom prices
- Execution Algorithms — Slice large parent orders into timed child orders to minimize market impact and slippage across benchmark, aggressive, and passive families
- FIX Protocol — Standardized tag=value messaging format for orders, cancels, fills, and market data; the lingua franca connecting firms to every major exchange and ECN
- Garbage Collection — Automatic memory reclamation in Java and Python that triggers stop the world pauses of milliseconds; why the hot path uses C++ or Rust instead
- Heartbeat — Periodic keep alive message confirming a connection is live; its real job is detecting silent disconnects while resting orders remain exposed on the exchange
- Hot Path — The code executing on every tick where nanoseconds decide queue position; ruthlessly optimized with no allocation, logging, or system calls
- Implementation Shortfall — Perold's gap between the decision price and realized execution, capturing market impact, timing drift, spread, fees, and opportunity cost from unfilled quantity
- Jitter — Variance in latency measured as P99 minus P50; the freeze spikes are exactly when fast competitors snipe your stale quotes
- Latency — Round trip delay between sending a message and receiving acknowledgement; physics floors wire latency at 5 microseconds per kilometer, so only colocation truly fixes it
- Latency Units — The millisecond, microsecond, and nanosecond scales for measuring system speed; knowing which applies to each stage prevents mismatched architecture and budgets
- Memory Allocation — Reserving RAM at runtime; malloc costs 100 to 500 ns with unpredictable tail spikes, so hot paths pre allocate object pools at startup
- POV — Execution algo that trades a fixed fraction of real time market volume, speeding up when active and slowing when quiet without needing a volume forecast
- Smart Order Routing — Automatically splits each order across fragmented venues by price, depth, fees, latency, and fill probability; decides where while execution algos decide when
- TCA — Post trade measurement of execution quality against VWAP, arrival, or close benchmarks; decomposes cost into spread, impact, timing, and opportunity to grade algos, brokers, and venues
- Throughput — Messages per second a system can process, distinct from per message latency; insufficient capacity queues messages during volatility spikes and produces stale quotes
- Tick to Trade — Total time from a market data update hitting the NIC to the resulting order leaving it; the single metric that sets passive queue position
- TWAP — Execution algorithm that slices an order into equal pieces at fixed time intervals ignoring volume; preferred for lumpy commodity liquidity and 24 hour FX where it resists gaming
- VWAP — Volume weighted average price serving as both the standard institutional execution benchmark and an algorithm that paces child orders proportional to expected volume per bucket
