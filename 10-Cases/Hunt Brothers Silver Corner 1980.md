# Hunt Brothers Silver Corner 1980

The Hunt brothers took delivery of so much silver that shorts could not find metal to deliver, until rule changes, scrap supply and margin calls broke the corner on Silver Thursday, 27 March 1980.

- **Who:** Nelson Bunker Hunt and William Herbert Hunt, with Saudi partners, against shorts that were mostly hedgers such as refiners, industrial users and the Peruvian marketer Minpeco
- **When:** 1979 to March 1980
- **The trade:** long COMEX and CBOT silver futures, taking delivery instead of rolling and holding the metal off the market, financed by borrowing against the silver itself
- **Why it paid or failed:** silver went from about 6 $/oz in early 1979 to about 50 $/oz in January 1980. Then COMEX raised margins, capped positions and moved to liquidation only, so no new longs and no rolls; scrap from melted silverware raised deliverable supply; and the falling price cut the value of the collateral while margin calls grew, under Volcker's credit squeeze. Silver was near 11 $/oz on Silver Thursday, and banks lent the Hunts about 1 bn $ against family oil assets for an orderly unwind
- **The concept behind it:** [[Squeeze and Corner]], [[Margin]], [[Backwardation Squeeze]], [[Silver Futures]], [[COMEX]]

## Lessons for a desk

- The exchange is part of the counterparty risk: its rulebook can change mid position.
- Borrowing against the asset you are long is wrong way risk: collateral and exposure fall together.
- Very high prices pull out supply nobody counted.
- A squeeze shows first in the front spread against later months.
