# Probo Koala 2006

Trafigura bought cheap high sulphur coker naphtha to clean and blend into gasoline on board a ship, and the toxic waste left by the process was dumped in Abidjan in August 2006.

- **Who:** Trafigura, with a local waste contractor in Abidjan, Côte d'Ivoire
- **When:** 2006, waste discharged in August 2006
- **The trade:** buy Mexican coker naphtha, a sulphur laden gasoline component refiners avoided, wash it with caustic soda on the tanker Probo Koala, blend it to spec and sell the gasoline into West Africa
- **Why it paid or failed:** the quality spread looked wide because the ingredient's true cost, safe disposal of the washing waste, was not in its price. After the dumping tens of thousands sought medical treatment; Trafigura paid about 200m $ to the Ivorian government in 2007 and settled a UK group claim in 2009 while denying liability for deaths, and a Dutch court fined it in 2010
- **The concept behind it:** [[Three Transformations]], [[Blending]], [[Quality Spread]], [[Operational Risk]]

## Lessons for a desk

- A quality spread that looks too wide usually hides a cost you have not priced.
- Blending sits close to legal and environmental limits; the cost of crossing them dwarfs the margin.
- Reputation is part of the balance sheet of a trading house.
