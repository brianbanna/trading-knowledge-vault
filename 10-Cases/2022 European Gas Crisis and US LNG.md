# 2022 European Gas Crisis and US LNG

Russia cut pipeline gas to Europe through 2022, and LNG cargoes already in the system were the only fast replacement, so holders of flexible US offtake captured the gap between TTF and Henry Hub.

- **Who:** holders of US FOB offtake with free destination, including trading houses with LNG books; the liquefaction plants on the other side earned only their fees
- **When:** 2022, with TTF peaking around 340 €/MWh in August against Henry Hub near 8 to 9 $/MMBtu
- **The trade:** pay about 115% of Henry Hub plus a fixed liquefaction fee, ship the cargo to Europe and sell it on TTF
- **Why it paid or failed:** no new liquefaction could enter for years, so the gap stayed open and went to the contract holders. It failed for Freeport offtakers when the June 2022 fire shut the plant for months: the option vanished, and anyone who had sold those cargoes forward had to buy replacements at crisis prices
- **The concept behind it:** [[Sources of Trading House Edge]], [[Offtake Agreement]], [[Henry Hub-TTF Spread]], [[Force Majeure]]

## Lessons for a desk

- The edge was holding a cheap, flexible contract before the crisis, not forecasting it.
- A gap closes only as fast as new supply can enter; liquefaction takes 4 to 5 years.
- An asset is an option that can disappear overnight: a plant outage turns a long cargo position into a short.
