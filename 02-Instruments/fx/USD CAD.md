# USD/CAD

## Definition

USD/CAD, the "loonie", is quoted as the number of Canadian dollars per 1 US dollar. This is the inverse of the AUD/USD and [[EUR/USD]] convention where the USD sits in the quote position, so a higher USD/CAD print means a stronger dollar and a weaker CAD. On 2026-07-03 it traded 1.4168. The pair prices the [[WTI Crude Oil]] complex and the [[WCS|Western Canada Select]] discount, the [[Bank of Canada]] versus [[Federal Reserve]] rate differential, and, currently, policy risk from [[USMCA]] moving into its scheduled joint review rather than an automatic renewal.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

Canada is a major crude exporter, so CAD is a classic [[Petrocurrency]] and [[Commodity Currencies|commodity currency]]. When [[WTI Crude Oil]] rallies, Canada's [[Terms of Trade]] improve and CAD tends to strengthen, which shows up as USD/CAD falling. The relationship is noisier than the AUD to metals link for two reasons. First, most Canadian crude is landlocked heavy grade that sells at the [[WCS]] discount to WTI, so the discount matters as much as the flat price. Second, deep integration with the US economy means USD/CAD often trades the two central banks and the broad dollar more than oil on any given day. For a commodity trader, short USD/CAD is a liquid way to express bullish crude with a [[Bank of Canada]] rates overlay, but you must watch the [[Brent-WTI Spread]] and the WCS differential, not just the front WTI print.

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** [[WTI Crude Oil]] breaks higher from 71 to 74 USD on a supply disruption and the [[WCS]] discount narrows. A trader sells USD/CAD (long CAD) 10 standard lots (1,000,000 USD) at 1.4210, targeting a move toward 1.4150. Note that because USD is the base here, one pip of 0.0001 on a 100K USD lot is worth 10 CAD, not 10 USD; at 1.4168 that is about 7.06 USD per pip per lot. Spot falls to 1.4150 and the trader covers: 60 pips × 10 CAD × 10 lots = 6,000 CAD, roughly 4,235 USD. The fail case: the trader stays long CAD into a dovish [[Bank of Canada]] cut while crude reverses and the WCS discount blows out. USD/CAD rallies from 1.4150 to 1.4225 and the stop triggers, 75 pips × 10 CAD × 10 lots = 7,500 CAD, roughly 5,290 USD of loss. The oil view was right in direction but the rates and differential overlay dominated.

**Simplified:** USD/CAD is how many Canadian dollars buy 1 US dollar, so higher means a weaker CAD. It falls when oil is strong and the [[Bank of Canada]] is hawkish relative to the [[Federal Reserve]], and it rises when oil is weak, in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]], or when the broad dollar rallies. One pip is 0.0001. Because USD is the base, a pip on a 100K lot is worth about 10 CAD (near 7 USD at current spot), not a clean 10 USD.

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of CAD strength (USD/CAD lower)
- Rising [[WTI Crude Oil]] and a narrowing [[WCS]] discount (positive [[Terms of Trade]] shock)
- [[Bank of Canada]] hawkish relative to [[Federal Reserve]] (rate differential favors CAD)
- [[Risk-On Risk-Off|Risk on]] environments and strong global growth
- Constructive [[USMCA]] review outcome that removes trade uncertainty
- Broad USD weakness ([[DXY]] lower)

### Drivers of CAD weakness (USD/CAD higher)
- Falling crude and a widening [[WCS]] discount (negative [[Terms of Trade]] shock)
- [[Federal Reserve]] hawkish relative to [[Bank of Canada]] (US rate advantage widens)
- [[USMCA]] review friction, tariff threats, or pipeline and takeaway constraints
- [[Risk-On Risk-Off|Risk off]] and USD safe haven demand
- Weak Canadian labour or CPI data pulling forward [[Bank of Canada]] cuts

### Seasonality
Modest. USD/CAD can soften (CAD firmer) into the North American summer driving season when crude demand and refining runs peak, and firm in year end USD funding periods. The [[Bank of Canada]] versus [[Federal Reserve]] policy path and the oil differential dominate any seasonal tilt.

## Key data and reports

- [[Bank of Canada]] rate decisions, Monetary Policy Report, and Governor communications
- Canadian CPI (monthly, high impact for the BoC path)
- Canadian labour force survey (monthly employment)
- [[WTI Crude Oil]] prices, the [[WCS]] discount, and the [[Brent-WTI Spread]]
- EIA and Canadian crude inventory and production data
- US Non Farm Payrolls and [[Federal Reserve]] decisions
- [[USMCA]] joint review headlines and trade policy news
- [[CFTC]] IMM positioning: net speculative CAD positions

## Key spreads and relative value

- **USD/CAD vs 2 year rate differential (US 2Y minus CA 2Y):** the primary short term rates driver. US advantage widens = USD/CAD higher.
- **USD/CAD vs [[WTI Crude Oil]] and the [[WCS]] discount:** the [[Petrocurrency]] channel. Watch the discount, not just flat price.
- **CAD/JPY cross:** a [[Carry Trade]] and [[Risk-On Risk-Off]] expression that isolates CAD from the USD leg.
- **AUD/CAD cross:** metals versus oil commodity currency relative value. See [[AUD/USD]].
- **[[DXY]] (Dollar Index):** CAD carries roughly a 9% DXY weight, so broad dollar moves feed directly into the pair.

## Important relationships

- Inverse correlation with [[WTI Crude Oil]] (USD/CAD falls when crude rallies)
- Sensitive to the [[WCS]] discount and the [[Brent-WTI Spread]], not only flat crude
- Tight linkage to US front end yields given deep economic integration
- Positive correlation with the broad USD in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] episodes
- Event risk clustered around [[Bank of Canada]] and [[Federal Reserve]] meetings and [[USMCA]] review milestones
