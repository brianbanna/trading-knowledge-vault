# USD/CHF

## Definition

USD/CHF quotes how many Swiss franc buy 1 US dollar. Nicknamed the swissie, it is a G10 pair that pits two of the market's main safe haven currencies against each other. The franc is a classic low yielder and haven, so it tends to strengthen when global risk sells off and to weaken when risk recovers. A higher USD/CHF means a weaker franc. The pair is heavily shaped by the [[SNB]], which is an unusually active intervener and has run negative policy rates for long stretches to lean against franc strength.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

USD/CHF is a haven versus haven pair, which makes it a clean read on relative safe haven demand and on the [[SNB]] reaction function rather than on any commodity export story. For a commodity trader the franc matters in two ways. First, it is correlated with [[Gold Futures|gold]]: both are stores of value that attract flows during monetary stress and inflation fear, so the franc often moves with the metal. Second, the franc is one of the primary low yield funding currencies for the [[Carry Trade]]: investors borrow cheap francs to buy higher yielding [[Commodity Currencies|commodity]] and [[EM Currencies|EM]] currencies, so franc strength in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] episodes is partly the mechanical unwind of those funded carry positions. That funding role links the swissie to the same risk cycle that drives commodity currencies, just from the opposite side.

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** A global risk off session: a geopolitical shock hits and equities gap lower. The franc is bid as a haven, so USD/CHF falls (franc strengthens) from 0.8050 to 0.7920 within a day, a 1.6% move, while [[Gold Futures]] rallies in sympathy. A trader who was short USD/CHF (long franc) as a haven hedge gains roughly $80,000 on $5M notional, and the position also offsets losses on funded [[Carry Trade|carry]] longs elsewhere in the book. The fail case is the [[SNB|Swiss National Bank]] reaction function: two weeks later the franc has strengthened so much that the SNB intervenes in the market and signals discomfort with franc appreciation. USD/CHF jumps from 0.7900 back to 0.8080 in two sessions, a 2.3% reversal, and the trader who stayed long franc gives back more than the initial haven gain. Trading the swissie means trading against a central bank that actively leans against franc strength.

**Simplified:** USD/CHF is how many Swiss franc buy 1 dollar. A lower number means a stronger franc. The franc is a safe haven and a low yielder, so it strengthens when the world is scared (risk off) and weakens when calm returns. It tends to move with gold and is used to fund carry trades. The big wildcard is the [[SNB]], which actively intervenes and has used negative rates to stop the franc from getting too strong. Watch [[SNB]] policy, [[Gold Futures]], and global risk sentiment.

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of CHF strength (USD/CHF lower)
- Global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] and haven demand (geopolitics, equity selloffs)
- [[Carry Trade]] unwinds forcing buybacks of funding francs
- [[Gold Futures]] rallies during monetary and inflation stress
- [[SNB]] hawkish or ending negative rates
- European stress that also pushes [[EUR/CHF]] lower
- [[DXY]] weakness and a softer global dollar

### Drivers of CHF weakness (USD/CHF higher)
- Global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk on]] reducing haven demand
- [[SNB]] intervention selling francs and dovish, negative rate policy
- Fed hawkish relative to the [[SNB]], widening the dollar rate advantage
- Renewed [[Carry Trade]] risk appetite funded in francs
- [[DXY]] strength
- Falling gold prices

### Seasonality
Little reliable seasonality. The franc trades on the global risk regime and the [[SNB]] reaction function, both of which override calendar effects. Year end USD funding demand can lift USD/CHF at the margin, but haven flows dominate.

## Key data and reports

- [[SNB]] quarterly monetary policy assessments and intervention signals
- SNB sight deposit data (a proxy for intervention activity)
- Swiss CPI and inflation prints
- FOMC decisions and the US rate path (the dollar leg)
- [[Gold Futures]] prices and haven demand indicators
- Global risk proxies: [[VIX]], equity futures, geopolitical headlines
- Swiss current account and trade data (structural surplus supports the franc)
- CFTC IMM positioning in Swiss franc futures

## Key spreads and relative value

- **USD/CHF vs [[Gold Futures]]:** both haven stores of value; the franc often tracks gold during monetary stress.
- **[[EUR/CHF]] cross:** the European stress gauge. Falls during Eurozone sovereign or banking stress, and a key [[SNB]] focus.
- **USD/CHF vs [[USD/JPY]]:** the two main funding and haven currencies. Relative value separates Swiss haven flows from Japanese haven and rate driven flows.
- **CHF vs the US 2 year rate differential:** the rate leg driver, though haven flows and SNB policy frequently override it.

## Important relationships

- Positive correlation with [[Gold Futures]] as a fellow store of value
- Inverse correlation with global risk appetite (franc strengthens in risk off)
- Franc as a primary [[Carry Trade]] funding currency, linking it to the risk cycle
- Heavy influence from [[SNB]] intervention and negative rate policy
- Tight relationship with [[EUR/CHF]] given Switzerland's trade and financial links to the Eurozone
- Correlation with the US rate differential via the dollar leg
