# USD/CLP

## Definition

USD/CLP quotes how many Chilean peso buy 1 US dollar. It is the cleanest copper proxy currency in the FX market. Chile produces roughly a quarter of the world's mined copper, so its export receipts, terms of trade, and the peso move tightly with the copper price and with Chinese demand for the metal. A higher USD/CLP means a weaker peso. The peso is not fully deliverable offshore, so most external exposure trades through non deliverable forwards.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

Copper is Chile's dominant export, so the peso is the purest [[Commodity Currencies|commodity currency]] expression of the copper cycle. When [[Copper Futures]] rallies, Chilean export receipts and terms of trade improve and the peso firms; when copper falls, the peso weakens almost one for one in trend terms. For a commodity trader USD/CLP is a liquid, lower cost way to express a copper and Chinese industrial demand view, and it often leads or confirms moves in the metal. Because copper demand is dominated by China, [[China PMI]] and Chinese property and grid investment are first order drivers of the peso. Chile is also emerging as a major lithium producer, adding a battery metals dimension to the currency. The peso carries a moderate [[Carry Trade]] premium over the dollar via [[BCCh]] policy, smaller than the [[USD/BRL|real]] or [[USD/ZAR|rand]] but still a factor in [[EM Currencies|EM]] positioning.

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** A trader is bullish copper on tightening mine supply and a Chinese grid investment push, and expresses it through short USD/CLP (long peso) at 930 rather than through the metal, to pick up carry and avoid futures roll cost. The win case: [[Copper Futures]] rallies 8% over a month as [[China PMI]] beats and inventories draw. USD/CLP falls to 892, a 4.1% spot gain plus a small positive carry, roughly $215,000 on $5M notional. The fail case: Chinese property data disappoints, copper reverses 6%, and a domestic political shock (a constitutional or royalty proposal that threatens mining margins) hits at the same time. USD/CLP jumps from 895 to 948 over five sessions, a 5.9% loss that combines the copper leg with Chile specific mining policy risk. The peso is a clean copper proxy on most days, but domestic mining and political risk can decouple it from the metal at exactly the wrong time.

**Simplified:** USD/CLP is how many Chilean peso buy 1 dollar. A higher number means a weaker peso. It falls (peso strengthens) when copper rallies and Chinese demand is strong; it rises (peso weakens) when copper falls, when China slows, and when the dollar is strong. Because Chile mines about a quarter of the world's copper, the peso is the market's cleanest copper proxy. Watch [[Copper Futures]], [[China PMI]], and [[BCCh]].

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of CLP strength (USD/CLP lower)
- [[Copper Futures]] rallies improving terms of trade
- Strong Chinese demand ([[China PMI]], property, grid investment)
- [[BCCh]] hawkish, widening the carry advantage over the dollar
- Global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk on]] and [[EM Currencies|EM]] inflows
- [[DXY]] weakness
- Rising lithium export revenue and stable mining policy

### Drivers of CLP weakness (USD/CLP higher)
- Falling copper prices and weak Chinese industrial demand
- Domestic political and mining policy risk (royalties, constitutional change, permitting)
- Global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] and broad [[EM Currencies|EM]] selloff
- [[DXY]] strength
- [[BCCh]] dovish, compressing the carry advantage
- Copper supply gluts or Chinese property downturns

### Seasonality
Weak standalone seasonality; the copper price and the Chinese demand cycle dominate. Chinese demand tends to strengthen around construction seasons, which can indirectly support the peso, but the metal's cycle and the global risk regime overwhelm calendar effects.

## Key data and reports

- [[Copper Futures]] and LME copper prices and inventory data
- [[China PMI]] and Chinese property, credit, and grid investment indicators
- [[BCCh|Banco Central de Chile]] interest rate decisions
- Chile CPI inflation prints
- Chile copper export volumes and trade balance
- Codelco and major mine production and disruption news
- Lithium prices and Chilean lithium export policy
- Global risk proxies: [[VIX]], [[EM Currencies|EM]] FX index, [[DXY]]

## Key spreads and relative value

- **USD/CLP vs [[Copper Futures]]:** the core relationship. The peso is the cleanest FX copper proxy, so the pair and the metal trend together.
- **USD/CLP vs [[China PMI]]:** Chinese demand is the dominant copper driver, so the peso is effectively a China industrial demand trade.
- **CLP vs [[USD/BRL]]:** both Latin American commodity currencies, but CLP is copper driven while BRL is ag and fiscal driven, separating the two commodity stories.
- **USD/CLP vs [[AUD/USD]]:** both are metals proxies (copper for CLP, iron ore and broad metals for AUD), useful for relative value on the China metals complex.

## Important relationships

- Strong inverse correlation with the [[Copper Futures|copper]] price (peso strengthens as copper rises)
- Tight link to Chinese demand via [[China PMI]] and property and grid investment
- Positive correlation with [[DXY]] and with global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]]
- High beta to the broad [[EM Currencies|EM]] FX complex
- Sensitivity to domestic mining and political risk, which can decouple the peso from copper
- Emerging lithium export channel adding a battery metals dimension
