# USD/NOK

## Definition

USD/NOK, the "nokkie", is quoted as the number of Norwegian kroner per 1 US dollar, so a higher print means a weaker NOK. On 2026-07-03 it traded 9.8159. NOK is a [[Brent Crude]] linked [[Petrocurrency]] driven by [[Norges Bank]] policy and, structurally, by flows around the sovereign wealth fund, the [[GPFG|Government Pension Fund Global]]. It is a smaller, thinner, higher beta currency than the majors, which makes it move faster in both directions and sell off sharply in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]].

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

NOK is Europe's cleanest liquid oil currency. Norway is a large [[Brent Crude]] and gas exporter, so higher energy prices lift the country's [[Terms of Trade]] and, all else equal, support the krone (USD/NOK lower). Two features make it distinct from CAD as a [[Petrocurrency]]. First, liquidity is thin relative to the majors, so spreads are wider, moves are larger, and the currency behaves as a high beta [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk]] proxy: it rallies in calm risk on tapes and gaps weaker when volatility spikes, sometimes decoupling from the oil print in a panic. Second, the [[GPFG]] and the government's use of oil revenue create recurring, semi predictable FX flows that the market tracks closely. For a commodity trader, USD/NOK is a fast, higher volatility way to express an energy and European risk view, but position sizing must respect the thinner book and the [[Norges Bank]] reaction function.

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** A [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] equity selloff hits and [[Brent Crude]] drops 4 USD in two sessions. A trader buys USD/NOK (short the krone) 10 standard lots (1,000,000 USD) at 9.8000, expecting the high beta krone to underperform. Note that because USD is the base and NOK trades near 9.8, a pip of 0.0001 on a 100K USD lot is worth 10 NOK, roughly 1.02 USD, and traders more often watch the "big figure" of 0.01. Spot spikes to 9.9200, a move of 0.1200 or 1,200 pips: 1,200 × 10 NOK × 10 lots = 120,000 NOK, roughly 12,220 USD, though wider spreads in the thin market shave the fill. The fail case: the trader instead shorts USD/NOK (long krone) as a [[Carry Trade]] for the yield, but [[Brent Crude]] tanks into a risk off wave and USD/NOK jumps from 9.8100 to 9.9500, a 1,400 pip loss of about 140,000 NOK, roughly 14,260 USD, made worse by slippage. The krone punishes carry longs in exactly the regime the carry looked most attractive.

**Simplified:** USD/NOK is how many Norwegian kroner buy 1 US dollar, so higher means a weaker krone. It falls when [[Brent Crude]] is strong, [[Norges Bank]] is hawkish, and risk appetite is high. It rises fast in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] because NOK is thin and high beta. One pip is 0.0001, worth about 10 NOK (near 1 USD) on a 100K USD lot, but the market usually thinks in 0.01 big figures given the price level.

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of NOK strength (USD/NOK lower)
- Rising [[Brent Crude]] and gas prices (positive [[Terms of Trade]] shock)
- [[Norges Bank]] hawkish relative to [[Federal Reserve]] (rate differential favors NOK)
- [[Risk-On Risk-Off|Risk on]] environments and calm volatility
- Supportive [[GPFG]] and government NOK buying flows
- Broad USD weakness ([[DXY]] lower)

### Drivers of NOK weakness (USD/NOK higher)
- Falling energy prices (negative [[Terms of Trade]] shock)
- [[Federal Reserve]] hawkish relative to [[Norges Bank]]
- [[Risk-On Risk-Off|Risk off]] and volatility spikes, where thin liquidity amplifies the move
- [[GPFG]] related NOK selling to fund fiscal spending
- Broad USD strength and European growth weakness

### Seasonality
Thin and flow driven rather than seasonal. The most reliable calendar effects come from [[GPFG]] and government FX transactions announced by [[Norges Bank]] and from month end and year end liquidity, when the thin book can exaggerate moves. Energy prices and the global risk regime dominate.

## Key data and reports

- [[Norges Bank]] rate decisions and the daily FX transaction schedule
- Norwegian CPI and core inflation (monthly)
- [[Brent Crude]] and European gas prices
- [[GPFG]] flow and allocation news
- Norwegian mainland GDP and Regional Network survey
- US Non Farm Payrolls and [[Federal Reserve]] decisions
- Global equity volatility and [[Risk-On Risk-Off]] sentiment

## Key spreads and relative value

- **USD/NOK vs [[Brent Crude]]:** the [[Petrocurrency]] channel; watch for decoupling in risk off panics.
- **EUR/NOK cross:** the domestic gauge most Norwegian corporates and [[Norges Bank]] focus on, stripping out the USD leg.
- **NOK/SEK cross:** oil krone versus the Swedish krona, a Scandi relative value and beta trade. See [[USD/SEK]].
- **USD/NOK vs 2 year rate differential (US 2Y minus NO 2Y):** the rates driver behind the flow story.
- **[[DXY]] (Dollar Index):** NOK is not in the basket, but broad dollar direction still sets the tone.

## Important relationships

- Inverse correlation with [[Brent Crude]] and European gas
- High beta to global [[Risk-On Risk-Off]]: outperforms in risk on, sells off hard in risk off
- Thinner liquidity than the majors means wider spreads and larger gaps
- Recurring, semi predictable flow pressure from the [[GPFG]] and government transactions
- Trades with EUR/NOK and the broader Scandi complex more than with the dollar bloc
