# AUD/USD

## Definition

AUD/USD, the "aussie", is the price of 1 Australian dollar in US dollars. It is among the five most traded pairs globally and the market's most liquid proxy for [[China]] linked industrial metals demand. On 2026-07-03 it traded 0.6939, up 0.68% on the day, rallying alongside [[Copper Futures]] (up 2.23%) after a softer US jobs report pulled [[Federal Reserve]] cut expectations forward. More precisely, AUD/USD compresses Australia's [[Terms of Trade]], the [[RBA]] versus [[Federal Reserve]] rate differential, and global risk appetite into a single liquid instrument.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

For a commodity trader the aussie is the cleanest liquid expression of the China industrial cycle. Australia exports [[Iron Ore]], copper, coal, and LNG, so AUD tends to strengthen when [[China PMI]] accelerates and the metals complex bids. It is the archetypal [[Commodity Currencies|commodity currency]]: its [[Terms of Trade]] rise and fall with bulk commodity prices, and the currency follows. AUD is also a [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk on]] currency and, depending on where the rate cycle sits, either a [[Carry Trade]] target or a funding leg. Because it trades nearly 24 hours and leads the Asia session, AUD/USD often front runs the metals tape and moves in near lockstep with [[NZD/USD]]. When you cannot get size in physical copper or iron ore, long AUD is the liquid substitute for the same macro view.

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** Friday, US Non Farm Payrolls miss by 90k and the prior month is revised down. AUD/USD was 0.6892 going into the print. A trader who wanted long China linked metals risk buys 10 standard lots (1,000,000 AUD) at 0.6905 as [[Copper Futures]] jump 2.23% and [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk on]] flows lift the aussie. Spot rallies to 0.6952 and the trader exits. In FX one pip is 0.0001, and on a standard 100K AUD lot that is roughly 10 USD per pip, so 47 pips across 10 lots is about 47 × 10 × 10 = 4,700 USD. The fail case is the same trade two sessions later: long 10 lots at 0.6940 into a weak [[China PMI]] print, a [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] wave hits, [[AUD/JPY]] and copper roll over together, and the position stops at 0.6895 for a 45 pip loss, about 4,500 USD. Same instrument, opposite regime.

**Simplified:** AUD/USD is how many US dollars buy 1 Australian dollar. It goes up when China and industrial metals are strong, when the [[RBA]] is hawkish relative to the [[Federal Reserve]], and when risk appetite is high. It goes down in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] and when metals sell off. One pip is 0.0001, and on a 100K AUD lot that is about 10 USD. A 100 pip move is roughly 1,000 USD per lot.

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of AUD strength (AUD/USD higher)
- [[RBA]] hawkish relative to [[Federal Reserve]] (rate differential moves in Australia's favor)
- Rising [[Iron Ore]], copper, coal, and LNG prices (positive [[Terms of Trade]] shock)
- Accelerating [[China PMI]] and Chinese fiscal or property stimulus
- [[Risk-On Risk-Off|Risk on]] environments and strong global equities
- [[Carry Trade]] demand when Australian yields sit above funding currencies

### Drivers of AUD weakness (AUD/USD lower)
- [[Federal Reserve]] hawkish relative to [[RBA]] (US rate advantage widens)
- Falling bulk commodity prices (negative [[Terms of Trade]] shock)
- China growth scare, property stress, or a weaker yuan fixing
- [[Risk-On Risk-Off|Risk off]] and USD safe haven bid ([[DXY]] higher)
- Carry unwind when volatility spikes and funding pairs are covered

### Seasonality
Weak and unreliable. The aussie has shown a mild tendency to firm in early Q1 on China restocking ahead of the Lunar New Year and to soften in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] quarters, but the China metals cycle and the [[RBA]] versus [[Federal Reserve]] rate path dominate any calendar effect.

## Key data and reports

- [[RBA]] cash rate decisions, statements, and minutes
- Australian CPI (quarterly and monthly indicator, high impact)
- Australian labour force survey (monthly employment and unemployment)
- [[China PMI]] (official NBS and Caixin), Chinese credit and activity data
- [[Iron Ore]] and copper spot and futures prices
- Australian trade balance and terms of trade releases
- US Non Farm Payrolls and [[Federal Reserve]] decisions
- [[CFTC]] IMM positioning: net speculative AUD positions

## Key spreads and relative value

- **AUD/USD vs 2 year rate differential (AU 2Y minus US 2Y):** the dominant short term rates driver. Differential narrows against Australia = AUD/USD lower.
- **AUD/NZD cross:** the pure Australia versus New Zealand trade, stripping out the USD. See [[NZD/USD]].
- **AUD/JPY cross:** the market's cleanest [[Risk-On Risk-Off]] and [[Carry Trade]] barometer. Falls hard in risk off.
- **AUD vs copper and iron ore:** AUD/USD tracks the bulk metals complex closely; divergences flag positioning or rate driven dislocations.
- **[[DXY]] (Dollar Index):** AUD is not in the DXY basket, but a broad dollar rally still pressures the pair.

## Important relationships

- Strong positive correlation with [[Copper Futures]] and [[Iron Ore]]
- High positive correlation with [[NZD/USD]] (the two commodity dollars trade together)
- Positive correlation with global equity risk sentiment ([[Risk-On Risk-Off]])
- Inverse correlation with the broad USD and, at the margin, with US 2 year yields
- Sensitive to [[China PMI]] and Chinese policy as a growth beta expression
