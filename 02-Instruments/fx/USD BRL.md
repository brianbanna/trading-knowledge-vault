# USD/BRL

## Definition

USD/BRL quotes how many Brazilian real buy 1 US dollar. It is the most important Latin American currency pair and a high yielding emerging market currency that trades on Brazil's commodity export flows, its very high policy rate, and its fiscal and political risk. A higher USD/BRL means a weaker real. Most offshore exposure trades through non deliverable forwards and cash settled futures rather than physical delivery, because Brazil retains capital account frictions.

Snapshot: 5.2051 on 2026-07-03. Brazil is a major exporter of soybeans, coffee, sugar, and iron ore, so the real tracks the agricultural and metals complex closely. The very high [[Selic Rate|Selic]] policy rate makes it a premier [[Carry Trade]] currency, and the pair is highly sensitive to domestic fiscal and political risk.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

Brazil is one of the world's largest exporters of [[Soybean Futures|soybeans]], [[Coffee Futures|coffee]], [[Sugar Futures|sugar]], and [[Iron Ore|iron ore]], so the real is a [[Commodity Currencies|commodity currency]] whose fair value moves with the agricultural and metals complex and with Chinese demand for those exports. When the soy and iron ore complex rallies, Brazil's export receipts and terms of trade improve and the real tends to firm. For a commodity trader the pair is a liquid macro overlay on the ag and metals cycle. The real is also a top [[Carry Trade]] currency: the [[BCB|Banco Central do Brasil]] has repeatedly run one of the highest real policy rates in the [[EM Currencies|EM]] world through the [[Selic Rate|Selic]], so holders of real are paid a large interest differential, which is why the pair attracts heavy carry positioning and unwinds sharply in [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]].

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** A trader runs a [[Carry Trade|carry]] long in real (short USD/BRL) to harvest the [[Selic Rate|Selic]] differential, entered at 5.05. The win case: over two months the [[Soybean Futures]] complex rallies on strong Chinese buying, [[Iron Ore]] firms, and the [[BCB]] signals rates stay high for longer. USD/BRL grinds to 4.88, a 3.4% spot gain on top of roughly 2% accrued carry over the period. On $5M notional that is about $270,000 combined. The fail case comes from the domestic risk channel that gold and metals cannot hedge: a surprise fiscal package raises the spending ceiling, the market prices looser policy and wider deficits, and USD/BRL gaps from 4.90 to 5.28 over four sessions, a 7.8% loss that erases more than a year of carry in a week. Brazil specific fiscal and political risk, not the commodity leg, is usually what breaks the carry.

**Simplified:** USD/BRL is how many real buy 1 dollar. A higher number means a weaker real. It falls (real strengthens) when soybeans, coffee, sugar, and iron ore rally and when the central bank keeps rates high. It rises (real weakens) when the world sells risk, when the dollar is strong, and above all when Brazil's fiscal or political outlook deteriorates. You are paid a very high interest rate to hold real, but fiscal surprises can wipe out that carry fast. Watch the [[BCB]], the [[Selic Rate|Selic]], and the ag and metals complex.

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of BRL strength (USD/BRL lower)
- Rallies in [[Soybean Futures]], [[Coffee Futures]], [[Sugar Futures]], and [[Iron Ore]] improving terms of trade
- [[BCB]] hawkish, keeping the [[Selic Rate|Selic]] high and widening the carry advantage
- Global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk on]] and strong [[EM Currencies|EM]] inflows
- [[DXY]] weakness
- Credible fiscal consolidation and political stability
- Strong Chinese demand for Brazilian ag and iron ore exports

### Drivers of BRL weakness (USD/BRL higher)
- Fiscal deterioration, spending ceiling breaches, rising deficit expectations
- Political risk and policy uncertainty (elections, central bank independence)
- Global [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk off]] and broad [[EM Currencies|EM]] selloff
- [[DXY]] strength
- Falling soybean, coffee, sugar, and iron ore prices
- Weak Chinese demand for Brazilian exports

### Seasonality
Agricultural export flows create mild seasonal patterns: the Brazilian soybean harvest and export window (roughly February to May) brings dollar inflows that can support the real, while the domestic fiscal calendar and year end USD funding pressure work the other way. The macro and fiscal regime dominates any seasonal tendency.

## Key data and reports

- [[BCB]] Copom interest rate decisions and the [[Selic Rate|Selic]] path
- Brazil IPCA inflation and IGP-M prints
- Fiscal data: primary balance, gross debt to GDP, spending ceiling news
- Soybean, coffee, sugar, and iron ore prices and export volumes
- Brazil trade balance and current account data
- Chinese demand indicators for ag and metals ([[China PMI]])
- Global risk proxies: [[VIX]], [[EM Currencies|EM]] FX index, US rates
- CFTC and B3 positioning in real futures

## Key spreads and relative value

- **USD/BRL vs the ag and metals complex:** soybeans, coffee, sugar, and iron ore as the terms of trade driver.
- **BRL carry vs [[DXY]]:** among the highest carry in liquid EM, but dollar strength and fiscal shocks overwhelm it.
- **USD/BRL vs [[USD/ZAR]] and [[USD/MXN]]:** high yield [[EM Currencies|EM]] basket. Relative value isolates Brazil specific fiscal risk from broad EM beta.
- **BRL vs [[USD/CLP]]:** both Latin American commodity currencies, but BRL is ag and fiscal driven while CLP is a copper proxy, so the pair separates the two commodity stories.

## Important relationships

- Positive correlation with the soybean, coffee, sugar, and iron ore complex through terms of trade
- Positive correlation with global risk appetite (real weakens in risk off)
- Positive correlation with [[DXY]] (dollar strength lifts USD/BRL)
- High beta to the broad [[EM Currencies|EM]] FX complex
- Strong sensitivity to domestic fiscal and political risk, often the dominant driver
- Sensitive to Chinese demand for Brazilian ag and iron ore exports
