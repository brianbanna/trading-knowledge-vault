# Soybean Meal Futures

## Definition

Soybean meal is the high protein solid left after crushing a soybean and extracting the oil. It is the dominant animal feed ingredient worldwide, used across poultry, hogs, cattle, and aquaculture, prized for its protein content of roughly 44 to 48%. Meal is one of the two outputs of the [[Crush Spread]] and is usually the larger of the two crush products by value. The benchmark contract is CBOT ZM, quoted in US dollars per short ton. Snapshot: 304.40 USD/short ton on 2026-07-02.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

Meal is the value anchor of the soybean complex. In most years it carries more of the [[Crush Spread|crush]] value than [[Soybean Oil Futures|oil]] does, so meal strength pulls [[Soybean Futures|beans]] higher and sets the tone for the crusher [[Process Margin|process margin]]. Because meal is a feed input, its price tracks the global protein cycle: hog and poultry margins, herd sizes, and the price of competing feeds. When crush margins are wide, crushers run plants hard, which produces meal and oil together and can flood the meal market even as demand is steady. That joint production dynamic makes meal and oil trade against each other inside the crush.

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** A US crusher locks in margin by selling the board crush. With November [[Soybean Futures|beans]] at 1,150 cents/bushel, December meal (ZMZ6) at 305 USD/short ton, and December [[Soybean Oil Futures|oil]] (ZLZ6) at 65 cents/lb, the per bushel product value is 6.71 USD of meal plus 7.15 USD of oil for a 2.36 USD gross margin. A drought scare cuts the South American crop, crushers there slow down, global meal supply tightens, and December meal rallies from 305 to 340 USD/short ton. Win case: a trader long meal against short oil inside a reverse crush captures the 35 USD move (ZM is 100 short tons, so 35 USD per ton is 3,500 USD per contract). Fail case: a bearish [[WASDE]] lifts US ending stocks, the [[Stocks to Use Ratio|stocks to use ratio]] climbs, and meal breaks 22 USD lower before the trader can roll, a 2,200 USD loss per contract, because feed demand is slow to absorb the extra supply.

**Simplified:** Soybean meal is what is left of the bean after you squeeze the oil out. It is the protein that animals eat, so its price follows how many hogs and chickens are being raised. Meal is usually the more valuable of the two things you get from crushing a bean, so when meal is strong the whole complex is strong. Each contract is 100 short tons, so a 0.10 USD move is 10 USD per contract.

## Fundamentals

### Supply drivers
- Joint production with [[Soybean Oil Futures|oil]]: every crushed bushel yields roughly 44 lbs of meal and 11 lbs of oil, so meal supply is set by crush volume
- Crush pace: wide [[Crush Spread]] margins pull more beans through plants and lift meal output
- Argentina: the world's largest meal and oil exporter, so Argentine crush policy and the peso drive the export meal balance
- US and Brazilian crush capacity expansion tied to renewable diesel demand for oil
- Substitute proteins: distillers grains (DDGS), canola meal, and fishmeal cap meal pricing power

### Demand drivers
- Global animal protein: poultry and hog feed rations set the base demand
- China and Southeast Asia hog herd recovery and aquaculture growth
- US domestic feed use, seasonally strongest in winter feeding
- Protein content premiums: high pro meal earns a premium over standard 44%

### Seasonality
Meal demand is firmest in the northern hemisphere winter feeding season, roughly November to March, while supply is heaviest during peak US crush after the fall harvest. The meal to oil value split inside the crush shifts through the year as biofuel demand pulls on oil in some months and feed demand pulls on meal in others.

## Key data and reports

- NOPA monthly crush: US crush volume and meal stocks, the key supply read
- USDA [[WASDE]]: meal production, use, and export balances
- USDA Export Sales (weekly): meal shipments and cancellations
- USDA Grain Crushings: monthly crush detail
- Argentine crush and export data
- CFTC COT report: managed money positioning in ZM

## Curve structure

The meal curve reflects the crush and feeding calendar rather than a storable crop cycle, since meal degrades and is not stored as long as beans. Tight nearby meal from strong crush margins and firm feed demand pushes the front into [[Backwardation]]. Ample crushed supply against soft feed demand gives carry [[Contango]]. Meal calendar spreads are watched alongside the [[Crush Spread]] because a change in crush pace hits meal and oil together.

## Key spreads and relative value

- **[[Crush Spread]]:** meal is one of the two output legs. Long beans versus short meal and oil is the board crush, the reverse is a reverse crush.
- **Meal to oil value share:** the split of crush value between meal and [[Soybean Oil Futures|oil]]. Meal usually holds the larger share, and the ratio swings with biofuel policy and feed demand.
- **Meal calendar spreads:** front versus deferred around winter feeding and peak crush.
- **Meal versus competing proteins:** meal against DDGS and canola meal on a protein adjusted basis.

## Important relationships

- Joint production with [[Soybean Oil Futures|oil]]: they are produced together, so they compete for the crush value split rather than moving independently
- Pulls [[Soybean Futures|beans]] higher when meal leads, since meal carries most crush value in most years
- Tracks the global protein and feed cycle: hog and poultry margins and herd size
- Argentine supply is the swing factor in the export meal market
- Inversely linked to the [[Stocks to Use Ratio|stocks to use ratio]] through the balance sheet: tight meal balances lift price and steepen backwardation
