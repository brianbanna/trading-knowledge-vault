# Soybean Oil Futures

## Definition

Soybean oil is the liquid vegetable oil extracted when a soybean is crushed, the second output alongside high protein [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]]. It is used in cooking and food processing, but its price is increasingly set by fuel, not food, because it is the main US biodiesel and renewable diesel feedstock. Oil is one of the two legs of the [[Crush Spread]]. The benchmark contract is CBOT ZL, quoted in US cents per pound. Snapshot: 65.56 cents/lb on 2026-07-02, down 7.41% on the week as the crush weakened.

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

Soybean oil is the crush product most exposed to energy and policy rather than to food demand. As the main US biodiesel and renewable diesel feedstock, its price is tied to biofuel margins and to policy: blending mandates, the Renewable Fuel Standard, and RIN-like credits that subsidize using vegetable oil as fuel. That link means oil competes with petroleum distillates through the bean oil heating oil (BOHO) relationship, which trades soybean oil against [[ULSD Heating Oil Futures|heating oil]] as a proxy for renewable diesel margin. Oil is also joint produced with [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]], so its swings inside the [[Crush Spread|crush]] shift the crusher [[Process Margin|process margin]] and pull on [[Soybean Futures|beans]].

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** Renewable diesel margins drive oil. In spring, a policy proposal to raise biomass based diesel blending volumes lifts expected feedstock demand. July [[Soybean Oil Futures|oil]] (ZLN6) rallies from 62 to 70 cents/lb over three weeks as renewable diesel plants bid for oil. Win case: a trader long oil against short [[ULSD Heating Oil Futures|heating oil]] in a BOHO trade captures the 8 cent move (ZL is 60,000 lbs, so 1 cent is 600 USD per contract, an 8 cent move is 4,800 USD per contract) as the renewable diesel margin widens. Fail case: the mandate is cut at the last minute, biofuel demand expectations collapse, and oil falls 7.41% in a week from 70.8 to 65.56 cents/lb, roughly 5.24 cents or 3,144 USD per contract, while the trader is still long and the [[Crush Spread]] weakens with it.

**Simplified:** Soybean oil is the oil you get when you crush a bean. People cook with it, but the price is mostly about fuel now, because it is turned into biodiesel and renewable diesel. So it moves with oil prices and with government biofuel rules. When the rules favor biofuel, soybean oil goes up. Each contract is 60,000 lbs, so a 0.01 cent per lb tick is 6 USD per contract.

## Fundamentals

### Supply drivers
- Joint production with [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]]: every crushed bushel yields roughly 11 lbs of oil and 44 lbs of meal, so oil supply is set by crush volume
- Crush pace: wide [[Crush Spread]] margins pull more beans through plants and lift oil output
- Competing vegetable oils: palm, rapeseed, and sunflower oil set the global edible oil balance and cap oil's pricing power
- Argentina: a major crush and oil exporter, so its policy and peso drive the export oil balance
- Crush capacity expansion built specifically to feed renewable diesel plants

### Demand drivers
- US biodiesel and renewable diesel: the swing demand factor, driven by blending mandates and margins
- Biofuel policy: the Renewable Fuel Standard, blender credits, and RIN-like credits
- Food and cooking oil demand, the traditional but now secondary driver
- Substitution: when soybean oil is expensive, buyers switch to palm and other oils

### Seasonality
Food demand for oil is relatively steady, so the seasonal signal comes more from crush pace and from the policy calendar than from a crop cycle. Peak US crush after the fall harvest raises oil supply, while biofuel demand and the annual mandate and credit decisions can override the physical [[Seasonality|seasonal]] pattern in any given year.

## Key data and reports

- NOPA monthly crush: US crush volume and soybean oil stocks, the key supply read
- EPA renewable fuel volume obligations and RIN-like credit prices
- USDA [[WASDE]]: oil production, use, and biofuel use balances
- EIA data on biodiesel and renewable diesel production and feedstock use
- USDA Export Sales (weekly): oil shipments
- CFTC COT report: managed money positioning in ZL

## Curve structure

The oil curve is shaped by crush pace and by the policy and energy calendar rather than by a simple storage cycle. Strong renewable diesel demand and firm crush margins can push the front into [[Backwardation]], while ample crushed supply against soft biofuel demand gives carry [[Contango]]. Because oil is joint produced with [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]], a shift in crush pace moves the whole [[Crush Spread]] and both product curves at once.

## Key spreads and relative value

- **[[Crush Spread]]:** oil is one of the two output legs alongside [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]]. It sets the oil share of the crusher [[Process Margin|process margin]].
- **Bean oil heating oil (BOHO):** soybean oil against [[ULSD Heating Oil Futures|heating oil]], the core renewable diesel margin proxy.
- **Oil to meal value share:** the split of crush value between oil and meal, which swings with biofuel policy versus feed demand.
- **[[Sugar-Ethanol Spread]]:** a parallel food versus fuel relationship in the softs complex, useful as an analog for how mandates redirect an agricultural product into energy.
- **Soybean oil versus palm oil:** the global edible oil substitution spread.

## Important relationships

- Joint production with [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]]: produced together, so they compete for the crush value split
- Tied to energy and policy through the BOHO relationship with [[ULSD Heating Oil Futures|heating oil]] and to biofuel mandates and credits
- Pulls [[Soybean Futures|beans]] and the [[Crush Spread]] when renewable diesel demand leads
- Competes with palm and other vegetable oils on the global edible oil balance
- More policy sensitive than [[Soybean Meal Futures|meal]]: a mandate change can move oil sharply while meal barely reacts
