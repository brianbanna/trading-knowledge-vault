# S&P 500

## Definition

The S&P 500 is a float weighted index of 500 large US companies and the broadest benchmark for US large cap equities. In plain terms, it is the number most people mean when they say "the market," and its level tracks the aggregate value of the largest listed US firms. Precisely, it is a market capitalization weighted index (weighted by free float shares), so a company's influence scales with its market value, and the megacap technology names carry the heaviest weights. Level 7,483.24 on 2026-07-02, up 20.17% over 1Y. It is the anchor of global risk sentiment and the primary [[Risk-On Risk-Off|risk on risk off]] barometer for a commodity and FX trader. It trades inversely to [[VIX]], its implied volatility index. The liquid futures expression is the CME E-mini (ES).

## Why it matters (commodities and FX)

The S&P 500 is the global risk gauge. When it rallies, capital rotates into risk assets and cyclical commodities, funding currencies weaken, and high beta FX (commodity and EM currencies) tends to strengthen. When it sells off, the opposite happens: a flight to [[Safe Haven Assets|safe havens]] bids the dollar, the yen, and [[Gold Futures|gold]], and risk sensitive commodities fall. For a commodity and FX trader who never touches a single stock, the S&P still matters because it is the cleanest real time read on aggregate risk appetite, and it drives the correlations that dominate cross asset positioning. The index is also tightly linked to rates: because equity valuations discount future earnings, a rising [[US 10Y Treasury|10Y yield]] lifts the discount rate and pressures valuations, while [[Central Bank Policy]] easing supports them. The relationship with [[VIX]] is mechanical and inverse; a spike in the VIX almost always coincides with an S&P drawdown, and the pair together define the [[Macro Regime|risk regime]].

## Concrete example

**Concrete:** FOMC Wednesday, 20:00 CET. The S&P 500 is at 7,450 and the ES front month is trading near the same level. The [[Central Bank Policy|Fed]] cuts 25 bps and the statement guides toward two more cuts than the market had priced. Risk rallies. The S&P jumps from 7,450 to 7,483 (up about 0.44%), the [[VIX]] falls from 15 to 13 (inverse move), and high beta FX like AUD and the [[EUR/USD|euro]] strengthen while [[DXY|the dollar]] softens. A desk long 20 ES contracts profits: the E-mini multiplier is $50 per point, so a 33 point gain on 20 contracts is 33 × $50 × 20 = $33,000. The win case: long risk into a dovish surprise. The fail case: had the Fed held rates and signaled higher for longer, the S&P would have dropped roughly 1.5% to about 7,340, the VIX would have spiked toward 19, and the same 20 lot long would have lost about (110 points × $50 × 20) = $110,000. Equity beta is unforgiving on the downside.

**Simplified:** The S&P 500 is an index of 500 big US companies, weighted so the largest ones count most. It is what people mean by "the stock market," and its direction is the market's mood ring: up means risk on, down means risk off. Even if you only trade commodities and currencies, you watch it because risk on and risk off drives everything else, the dollar, the yen, gold, and cyclical commodities all move with it. It goes the opposite way to the [[VIX]] fear gauge. You trade it through the E-mini (ES) futures contract, where each index point is worth $50.

## Fundamentals

### Drivers of higher S&P (risk on)
- Dovish [[Central Bank Policy]] (cuts, QE, easier financial conditions)
- Falling [[US 10Y Treasury|10Y yields]] lowering the discount rate
- Strong earnings and margin expansion, especially megacap tech
- Improving growth data and easing recession fear
- Compressing credit spreads and a falling [[VIX]]

### Drivers of lower S&P (risk off)
- Hawkish policy and rising real yields
- Growth scares, recession signals (see [[Yield Curve Slope]] inversion)
- Earnings misses and guidance cuts
- Geopolitical shocks and credit stress
- Spiking [[VIX]] and widening credit spreads

### Concentration
The index is heavily weighted toward a handful of megacap technology names, so a small number of stocks can drive the aggregate level. This concentration means the S&P can rise even when the median stock is flat, a divergence worth watching for risk read accuracy.

## Key data and reports

- FOMC decisions and [[Central Bank Policy|Fed]] guidance
- Quarterly earnings season (megacap results dominate)
- US CPI and PCE inflation prints (discount rate channel)
- Non Farm Payrolls and growth data
- [[VIX]] level and term structure
- Credit spreads (IG and HY) as a corroborating risk gauge

## Key spreads and relative value

- **S&P 500 vs [[VIX]]:** the mechanical inverse pair defining the risk regime.
- **S&P 500 earnings yield minus [[US 10Y Treasury|10Y yield]]:** the equity risk premium, a valuation gauge versus bonds.
- **S&P vs Nasdaq 100 (ES vs NQ):** growth versus broad market, a factor tilt trade.
- **S&P vs Russell 2000:** large cap versus small cap, a cyclical and liquidity read.
- **Cross asset:** S&P direction versus [[DXY]] and [[Gold Futures|gold]] maps the [[Risk-On Risk-Off]] rotation.

## Important relationships

- Inverse to [[VIX]] by construction
- The primary [[Risk-On Risk-Off]] barometer for cross asset positioning
- Inverse to the discount rate: rising [[US 10Y Treasury|10Y yields]] pressure valuations, easing supports them
- Positive correlation with high beta and commodity FX in risk on, inverse with [[Safe Haven Assets|safe havens]]
- A key input to the broader [[Macro Regime]] read
- Sensitive to [[Central Bank Policy]] through both the discount rate and liquidity channels
